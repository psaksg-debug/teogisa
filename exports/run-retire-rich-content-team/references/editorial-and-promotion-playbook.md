## Brief template

Produce a one-page brief with:

- reader and exclusion reader;
- one-sentence problem and promise;
- search intent and primary query;
- action after reading;
- risk level and sensitive claims;
- existing content and internal links;
- official source candidates and check date;
- outline and original-value element;
- CTA map and commercial disclosure;
- owner, approver, due time, and acceptance criteria;
- block, revise, and stop conditions.

## Evidence and writing rules

Prefer primary and official sources for unstable or consequential claims. Record the URL, claim, check date, and limitations. Rewrite information into a reader-specific explanation, example, table, or checklist; do not reproduce source wording.

For income content, separate:

`gross receipts + variable compensation - tools - contractors - refunds - unpaid invoices - tax reserve - labor time`

Label hypothetical numbers as examples. Use actual results only with evidence, period, sample, conditions, costs, and a warning that they do not establish typical results.

For benefits, labor, tax, health, finance, or platform policy, include the basis date, individual variation, and final official check route.

## Schedule pattern

For an urgent but nonbreaking article, use a 48-hour plan:

- T+0–4h: lock reader, angle, claims, sources, CTA, and product facts.
- T+4–12h: build the evidence pack.
- T+12–20h: draft copy, table, and checklist.
- T+20–28h: fact, safety, originality, and editorial review.
- T+28–36h: mobile, accessibility, image, metadata, and CTA review.
- T+36–40h: policy and commercial-terms approval.
- T+40–44h: schedule with at least a two-hour buffer.
- T+44–48h: publish and verify the real URL.

Recalculate dates from the current time and timezone. Do not preserve an expired date from an old handoff. If a required fact is missing, keep the draft and move the schedule; never fill the gap with a guess.

## SEO and CTA package

Create:

- primary, synonym, task, reality-check, cost, audience, and product-support queries;
- one recommended title plus two alternatives;
- SEO title, meta description, OG title and description;
- slug, category, tags, and image text;
- three to five helpful internal links;
- official-source links;
- mid-article and final CTA with different intent;
- disclosure before the first commercial CTA.

Keep information at least 80% of a commercial article. Prefer “view contents or sample” before “buy now.” State seller, price, format, contents, exclusions, update scope, delivery, refund, and contact terms near the destination.

## Promotion and measurement

Prepare channel-specific summaries after publication. Do not copy the full article into another platform. Use a reader problem, one insight, and one next action.

Suggested UTM fields:

- `utm_source`
- `utm_medium`
- `utm_campaign`
- `utm_content`

Useful events:

- `content_view`
- `content_scroll` at 50 and 90 percent
- `internal_link_click`
- `official_source_click`
- `product_cta_impression`
- `product_cta_click`
- `product_sample_view`
- `checkout_start`
- `purchase`
- `challenge_start`

Never send personal data or advertising-account identifiers in analytics events.

At 7 days, check indexing, discovered queries, content depth, internal-link use, CTA baseline, and safety complaints. At 28 days, assess search impressions and clicks, CTR only with adequate impressions, CTA-to-sample behavior, conversion only with adequate sample, refunds, misunderstanding, and policy changes. Mark small samples `data insufficient`, not failure.
